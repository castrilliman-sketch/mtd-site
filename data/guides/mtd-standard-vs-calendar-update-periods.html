Short answer: the choice only changes where each quarter ends. Standard update periods end on the 5th (5 July, 5 October, 5 January, 5 April); calendar update periods end on the month-end (30 June, 30 September, 31 December, 31 March). The deadlines are the same for both: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May. GOV.UK links calendar periods to accounts that run 1 April to 31 March. Software defaults to standard, and once you send your first quarterly update for a tax year, the choice is fixed for the rest of that year.

GOV.UK also says that if you have an agent, they can tell you which to use if you're not sure. HMRC's developer guide (written for software makers) says only customers whose accounting period starts on 1 April benefit from calendar periods, and that software should not let other customers make the change. Not every product enforces this: FreeAgent's help page says it won't restrict the choice whatever your accounting dates.

Why 31 March works: HMRC's developer guide says that from 2024 to 2025, accounting periods running 1 April to 31 March are treated as equivalent to 6 April to 5 April, removing small adjustments of a few days. For traders, HMRC's Business Income Manual explains the rule: where accounts end between 31 March and 4 April, profits for the days up to 5 April are treated as nil for that tax year and arise in the next one, unless the trader elects otherwise.

When the choice locks, and how it's made

  • Default is standard. GOV.UK says your software will be set to standard update periods, and checking your accounting period is one of the steps before you start creating digital records.
  • Before your first update. Calendar periods have to be selected in your software before you send your first quarterly update. After you send an update, GOV.UK says you can't change your update periods for the rest of that tax year.
  • In software, not HMRC online. HMRC's developer guide says the change can't be made through HMRC online services; it's done through software. It also says someone signing up late in the tax year can still make the change, as long as it's before their first quarterly update.
  • Per business. HMRC's July 2026 agent update tells agents to make the selection for each of a client's self-employment and property businesses.
  • It carries on. HMRC's developer guide says a calendar choice continues year on year until you change back to standard through software.
  • Switch years start differently. HMRC's developer guide says that in a year you change, the first period's start date reflects the type you used before. FreeAgent's help page describes this as a first calendar quarter starting on 6 April if you're switching to calendar, and a last quarter ending on 5 April if you're switching away.

How it looks in two products, from their own help pages:

  • FreeAgent: Taxes, then Income Tax, choose the tax year, "Edit tax year details", then "Calendar year quarters". FreeAgent says the option only appears once the tax year has started (for 2026 to 2027, not until 6 April 2026).
  • Sage 50 Accounts: Connected services, then MTD for Income Tax Settings, where "Update period type" can be set to Calendar or Standard before the first quarterly update. After that, Sage shows a message that the update period can't be changed once a quarterly update has been submitted for the tax year.

If the setting doesn't match your accounts

GOV.UK's adjustments page covers the main cases for accounts running 1 April to 31 March:

  • Started in 2026 to 2027 and selected calendar periods: no adjustment of this type after the end of the tax year.
  • Started in 2026 to 2027 and forgot to select calendar periods: adjustments after the end of that tax year, with further guidance to be published later.
  • Tested the service in 2025 to 2026 on calendar periods: income and expenses from 1 to 5 April 2025 are added to the 2025 to 2026 annual totals, because those volunteers' first period started on 6 April. GOV.UK says this isn't needed in later years if you stay on calendar periods.

GOV.UK's worked example for an accounting period ending on 3 April: accounts run 3 April 2026 to 2 April 2027, but calendar periods run 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, so the 2026 to 2027 annual totals remove 1 and 2 April 2026 and add 1 and 2 April 2027.

Source: GOV.UK, Adjust your self-employment and property income.

Things worth checking in your software

  • Which update period type each business (each self-employment, UK property and foreign property business) is set to before the first update of the tax year.
  • Whether your period type shows in the software's list of obligations or deadlines. GOV.UK says that after you send an update, the recent activity in your HMRC online account says "You sent a quarterly update".
  • If you're changing software during a tax year, which period type your updates have already been sent under, since it can't change until the next tax year. GOV.UK says you can change software at any time, but you may find it easier at the end of the tax year. See also spreadsheets and bridging software.
  • Late quarterly updates can earn penalty points after your first required year. See MTD penalty points explained.

What GOV.UK doesn't say

  • How to switch back from calendar to standard periods for a later tax year: GOV.UK's MTD guidance doesn't spell it out. HMRC's developer guide says it's done through software.
  • Where the first calendar period starts for every case: GOV.UK's table shows 1 April, but the text under it gives the third update as covering "6 April to 31 December". HMRC's developer guide explains that the start date depends on the period type you used the year before.
  • The adjustment guidance for people who forgot to select calendar periods in 2026 to 2027, or whose accounts end on other dates: GOV.UK says it will publish this later.
