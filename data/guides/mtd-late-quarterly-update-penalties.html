Short answer: if you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, late quarterly updates get no penalty points in your first required tax year. That's 2026 to 2027 if you started in April 2026, and 2027 to 2028 if you start in April 2027. After that, GOV.UK says each missed deadline is 1 point, and at 4 points you get a £200 penalty, plus another £200 for each further missed deadline. Late tax returns get points in every year, including your first.

GOV.UK pages read on 20 November 2026, linked under each section. HMRC updated its penalties guidance on 19 November 2026 to add the first-year rule for people starting from April 2027. This page sets out how the rules work; it doesn't say what to do in your own case.

The first-year easement: who gets it

  • Started from 6 April 2026 (qualifying income over £50,000): no points for late quarterly updates in 2026 to 2027. GOV.UK's own example says the first quarterly update point you could get is for missing 7 August 2027.
  • Starting from 6 April 2027 (over £30,000): GOV.UK now says the same first-year rule applies, so no points for late quarterly updates in 2027 to 2028. GOV.UK says points start after the last quarterly update deadline of your first year. It doesn't give a dated example for this group. On that rule, the first quarterly update point would be for the 7 August 2028 deadline.
  • Starting from 6 April 2028 (over £20,000): unknown. GOV.UK's penalties guidance only mentions the April 2026 and April 2027 groups.
  • Volunteers: GOV.UK says there are no penalties for missing quarterly update deadlines while you're volunteering. Once you have to use MTD, the April 2026 and April 2027 groups still get the first-year rule.

Three things the easement doesn't cover:

  • The tax return. GOV.UK says you will get a point if you miss the tax return deadline, including in your first year.
  • Sending the updates at all. GOV.UK says you still need digital records and quarterly updates before you can submit your tax return through software. Updates that are never sent hold up the return, and a late return gets a point.
  • Paying late. Late payment penalties and interest still apply.

One wording difference: on 20 November 2026, GOV.UK's Send quarterly updates page still said points apply "after the 2026 to 2027 tax year" and didn't mention the April 2027 group. The main penalties page and the sign-up page, both updated on 19 November 2026, include the April 2027 first-year rule. This page follows the main penalties guidance.

Sources: GOV.UK, Penalties for MTD for Income Tax and its change note of 19 November 2026; GOV.UK, Send quarterly updates; GOV.UK, Penalties for volunteers.

  • First year: GOV.UK says that in your first year of the new penalties you have 30 days from the due date to pay in full or contact HMRC to set up a payment plan, and after that it's 15 days. You only get the 30-day period once, so someone who volunteered first keeps 15 days. GOV.UK doesn't say clearly whether the day-15 percentage is still charged in your first year if a payment goes past day 30.
  • Payments on account: GOV.UK says late payment penalties don't apply to them.
  • Payment plans: GOV.UK says that if a payment plan is agreed and you keep to it, penalties are paused from the date you contacted HMRC.
  • Interest: charged from the first day a payment is late until it's paid in full. HMRC's late payment interest rate is base rate plus 4%. HMRC lists it at 7.75% from 9 January 2026, and 8.00% from 24 November 2026.

Removing points, appeals and reasonable excuse

  • Below the threshold: each point is removed automatically 24 months after the missed deadline.
  • At the threshold: all points are removed once you've met both conditions. You need to send updates and returns on time for 12 months, and send anything outstanding from the previous 24 months. GOV.UK says your HMRC online services account shows the date your points will be removed.
  • Exceptional cases: GOV.UK says HMRC may cancel a penalty or point, or remove all points, in some exceptional circumstances such as insolvency.
  • Letters and appeals: HMRC sends a letter for each penalty point, each £200 penalty and each late payment penalty. GOV.UK says you can appeal if you disagree, and the letter tells you how. Its general appeals page says you usually have 30 days from the date the penalty was issued.
  • Reasonable excuse: GOV.UK's examples of what may count include a close relative dying shortly before a deadline, an unexpected hospital stay, your computer or software failing while you prepare an online return, and problems with HMRC online services. Examples it says will not count include not getting a reminder from HMRC and finding HMRC's online system too difficult. GOV.UK hasn't published an MTD-specific list for quarterly updates.

Sources: GOV.UK, Penalties for MTD for Income Tax; GOV.UK, Disagree with a penalty; GOV.UK, Reasonable excuses.

If you become exempt, or you volunteered first

  • Exempt during 2026 to 2027: GOV.UK says you go back to the current Self Assessment penalties. (See who is exempt and how to apply.)
  • Exempt during 2027 to 2028: you stay on the new penalties. GOV.UK says "from April 2027, the new penalties will apply to everyone who submits a personal Self Assessment tax return". Your threshold drops from 4 to 2, and any points are reduced so you're no closer to it. GOV.UK's example: 3 points becomes 1.
  • Volunteers who later have to use MTD: the threshold rises from 2 to 4. GOV.UK says 1 existing point becomes 3, so you're still 1 point away. Volunteers can't go back to the old penalties once they've agreed to the new ones.

Sources: GOV.UK, Penalties for MTD for Income Tax; GOV.UK, Penalties for volunteers.

What to look for in software if deadlines worry you

GOV.UK says not getting a reminder from HMRC isn't a reasonable excuse. Some software features that may help with deadlines:

  • Deadline reminders and an "obligations" view. These show which update periods are open, due or overdue (standard or calendar; see the difference). HMRC's software finder doesn't record reminder features, so our directory can't filter on them. The provider's own site is where to check. Your HMRC online account also shows overdue and upcoming quarterly updates.
  • A clear submission receipt. GOV.UK says that after you send an update, your HMRC online account's recent activity shows "You sent a quarterly update". HMRC's 2 November 2026 press release says you can check your submission history there or in your MTD software.
  • Tax return as well as updates. The tax return gets points from your first year. HMRC lists some products as quarterly-updates-only, so the return would need another product or an agent. Products listed as submitting the tax return.
  • Every income source in one place. One point per deadline covers all your businesses. Each self-employment and property business still needs its own update, so it's worth checking the software handles all of yours.
  • Early sending. GOV.UK says you can send an update up to 10 days before the end of the period if you don't expect more transactions. Most software lets you send updates more often.
  • Spreadsheet users: bridging software sends the updates from your spreadsheet. See spreadsheets and bridging software. For low-cost options, see free MTD software and the catches.
