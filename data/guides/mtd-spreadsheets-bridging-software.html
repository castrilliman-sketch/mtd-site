Short answer: yes, you can keep your spreadsheet. GOV.UK says a spreadsheet can stay your record-keeping tool, but it can't send anything to HMRC on its own. You need HMRC-recognised software that connects to it, often called bridging software, to send your quarterly updates and submit your tax return. The main thing to check: HMRC lists only {{bridging_tr}} of {{bridging_n}} bridging products as able to submit the tax return as well as the quarterly updates.

The digital links rule, in plain English

If your records are in one product (your spreadsheet) and submissions go from another (bridging software), GOV.UK says the two must be digitally linked. You set this up when you start, or before you send an update or your tax return.

GOV.UK lists as a digital linkGOV.UK says you must not do
  • Linked cells or formulas in a spreadsheet
  • Emailing a spreadsheet so it can be imported into other software
  • Moving files on a memory stick for someone to import
  • XML or CSV import and export, downloading and uploading files
  • Automated data transfer or an API
  • Copy information by writing it out in another cell or other software
  • Use cut and paste, or copy and paste, to move records

GOV.UK says this applies once a digital record has been sent to HMRC in a quarterly update.

GOV.UK says you don't need to link:

  • other income that isn't self-employment or property, such as dividends or savings
  • software that doesn't create your self-employment or property records, such as a booking system or till
  • a product used only for the tax return; GOV.UK says it gets the data directly from HMRC
  • your records to a co-owner's, if you let a property jointly (see our jointly owned property guide)

If you use one product for everything, GOV.UK says there's nothing to link.

Source: GOV.UK, Create digital records.

What your spreadsheet needs to hold

  • Each record: the amount, the date, and a category. GOV.UK says MTD uses the same income and expense categories as Self Assessment.
  • Less detail if turnover is under £90,000: GOV.UK says sole traders below that level for a business only need to record whether each item is income or an expense. Landlords with residential property also need to mark expenses that are restricted finance costs.
  • One UK property business: all your UK lets count as one business, so one set of records is enough. Each sole trader business needs its own records and its own quarterly updates.
  • When: records for a quarter need to exist before you send that update or before its deadline, whichever comes first. GOV.UK suggests recording things as close to the date as possible.
  • Keep the paperwork: you still keep original records such as bank statements and invoices, and keep digital records for at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline for the tax year.

Quarterly updates are totals per category, not individual transactions. HMRC says it doesn't receive details of individual receipts or invoices. Dates and what goes in an update are on Do I need MTD?

Sources: GOV.UK, Create digital records; GOV.UK, Send quarterly updates.

Fixing a mistake

GOV.UK says to correct the record in your spreadsheet, then digitally link your records to your bridging software again. During the year, the fix flows into your next quarterly update. In HMRC's own myth-buster ahead of the 7 November 2026 deadline, it says there's "no need to resend quarterly updates". If you've already sent the fourth update, GOV.UK gives two routes: correct the record and resend the fourth update, or adjust the category total in your software and reflect it in your records.

Sources: GOV.UK, Create digital records; HMRC press release, 2 November 2026.

What to compare between bridging products

  • Tax return or updates only. {{bridging_tr}} of {{bridging_n}} bridging products list both on HMRC's finder.
  • Your spreadsheet. Excel, Google Sheets or a CSV upload: support varies, so check the provider's site.
  • Income types. Self-employment, UK property, foreign property (overseas lettings are a separate business under MTD; see MTD and foreign property).
  • How it's priced. Per year, per month, per submission or per taxpayer. HMRC lists {{bridging_free}} bridging products with a free version (see free MTD software and the catches).
  • Update periods. Standard (tax year) or calendar quarters, if your accounts run to 31 March.
