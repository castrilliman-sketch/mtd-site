Short answer: if your self-employment and property income before expenses was over £30,000 on your 2025 to 2026 tax return, you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2027. GOV.UK says people in this group "should sign up before 6 April 2027". It hasn't said whether HMRC will sign this group up for them.

Everything on this page comes from GOV.UK pages we read on 19 November 2026, linked under each section. Where GOV.UK doesn't say something, we say "unknown" or "GOV.UK hasn't said". This page sets out the options; it doesn't say which to choose.

Are you in the April 2027 group?

GOV.UK says you need to use MTD for Income Tax if all of these apply:

  • you're a sole trader or landlord registered for Self Assessment
  • you get income from self-employment or property, or both
  • your qualifying income is over the threshold for the tax year

Qualifying income is your total self-employment and property income before expenses (turnover). Your salary (PAYE), your share of partnership profit, dividends, the State Pension and private pensions don't count. HMRC's own example: £25,000 of rent plus £27,000 of self-employment income is £52,000 of qualifying income. If you let a property jointly, only your share counts (see our jointly owned property guide).

HMRC also has an online checker that asks about the 2024 to 2025, 2025 to 2026 and 2026 to 2027 tax years and tells you if and when you need to start: check if you need to use MTD for Income Tax.

Sources: GOV.UK, Find out if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax; GOV.UK, Work out your qualifying income.

How HMRC tells you

GOV.UK says HMRC reviews your Self Assessment tax return and checks your qualifying income each year. If it's over the threshold, HMRC will write to you confirming that you need to start by the beginning of the next tax year. For the April 2027 start, that's the 2025 to 2026 return, which is due by 31 January 2027.

Two points GOV.UK makes plainly:

  • No letter doesn't mean you're out. "If you did not receive a letter, it is still your responsibility to check if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax."
  • If you disagree that you need to use it, GOV.UK says to contact HMRC.

When HMRC will send letters to the April 2027 group is unknown: GOV.UK doesn't give a date.

Source: GOV.UK, Find out if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

GOV.UK points out a difference between the two routes. When you sign yourself up you can make sure your income sources are up to date and include your latest circumstances. When HMRC signs you up, it uses only the information it already holds, which "may not include any changes to your circumstances since you last submitted a tax return". People HMRC signed up are then asked to confirm their income sources, add new ones and report ones that have stopped.

If you use an agent, GOV.UK says they can sign you up instead.

Sources: GOV.UK, Sign up for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax; GOV.UK, Check what to do if HMRC has signed you up.

How signing up works

You sign up through HMRC's online service: Sign up for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (tax.service.gov.uk). HMRC checks you're eligible from the details you give.

To be able to sign up, GOV.UK says you must

  • be registered for Self Assessment
  • have submitted a tax return in the last 2 years

The service won't ask about your other income and gains (for example savings or a salary). GOV.UK says you add those in your software before you submit your tax return.

If your income is close to £30,000

  • The test is "over £30,000". £30,000 exactly isn't over. If you're at or below £30,000 for 2025 to 2026, the next check is over £20,000 on your 2026 to 2027 return, for April 2028.
  • It's turnover, not profit. Rent or takings before expenses count, so profit well under £30,000 can still mean turnover over it.
  • VAT on the cash basis. If you're VAT registered and use the cash basis, you can include or exclude VAT in your business income; if you include it, it counts.
  • A new sole trader business. HMRC annualises a sole trader's qualifying income if it has the information: six months' trading in the first year is doubled. Property income you annualise yourself.
  • Amending your return. A change before 6 April 2027 that takes you over £30,000 means you need to use MTD for 2027 to 2028; one that takes you under means you don't. After 6 April 2027, a change that takes you under means you don't need to for that year (you can stay on voluntarily or opt out); a change that takes you over isn't considered for that year.
  • Stopped income. If one source stopped but you still have another, the stopped one still counts. If all your self-employment and property income stopped, GOV.UK says to tell HMRC before the start of the next tax year, otherwise you'll need to use MTD.
  • Dropping below later. Once you're using MTD, GOV.UK says you can choose to opt out if your qualifying income is below the threshold for 3 tax years in a row.

Sources: GOV.UK, Work out your qualifying income; GOV.UK, Sign up.

Exemptions, briefly

If you're exempt you don't sign up, but you still send a Self Assessment tax return. Some exemptions are automatic: for example qualifying income of £20,000 or less, no National Insurance number before the start of the tax year, and partnerships (for now). Others you apply for, such as being digitally excluded (it isn't reasonable for you to use software because of, for example, age, health, disability, location or religious beliefs). GOV.UK says HMRC won't accept an application whose only reason is being unfamiliar with software, having few records, or extra time or cost.

Several temporary exemptions (for example for averaging relief, qualifying care relief, or the SA107 or SA109 pages) last until April 2027. GOV.UK says people with those exemptions will need to use MTD from 2027 to 2028 if their qualifying income is above £30,000 in 2025 to 2026. The full list is on GOV.UK, Find out if you can get an exemption. Our MTD exemptions guide covers the full list, how to apply by phone or letter, and appeals.
