Short answer: if you're UK tax resident, rent from property abroad counts towards your MTD qualifying income alongside UK rent and self-employment income, before expenses. Under MTD, all your overseas lettings form one "foreign property business" that is separate from your UK property business, so it gets its own quarterly updates. You keep records for each foreign property, but send one update for all of them. Not every product handles this: {{fp_n}} of the {{products_total}} products on HMRC's software finder list foreign property.

Does foreign rent count towards the threshold?

It depends on where you were tax resident. GOV.UK describes this using the 2024 to 2025 tax year (the return HMRC used for the April 2026 start):

Your positionWhat counts as qualifying incomeWhat doesn't countGOV.UK's example
UK tax residentSelf-employment income plus UK and foreign property income, before expensesSalary (PAYE), your share of partnership profit, dividends, State Pension, private pensionsA sole trader in the UK who rents out a property in France: both incomes count
Not UK tax residentUK property income, self-employment income declared on your UK Self Assessment return, and income from a trade of dealing in or developing UK landForeign property income, and self-employment income not declared on your UK returnSomeone tax resident in Spain who rents out a UK property and is a sole trader in Spain: only the UK rent counts

The thresholds are the same as for everyone: over £50,000 (from 6 April 2026), over £30,000 (from 6 April 2027) and over £20,000 (from 6 April 2028). See Do I need MTD, and when?

An illustration (our numbers, using GOV.UK's rules): a UK resident with £18,000 of UK rent and £14,000 of rent from a flat abroad, both before expenses, has qualifying income of £32,000. On a 2025 to 2026 return, that's over the £30,000 threshold for April 2027. Had the same person been non-resident, only the £18,000 of UK rent would count.

If you let a foreign property jointly, only your share counts, as with UK property (see our jointly owned property guide).

Source: GOV.UK, Work out your qualifying income (section "How your tax residence in the 2024 to 2025 tax year affects your qualifying income"); GOV.UK, Find out if and when you need to use MTD for Income Tax.

The SA109 exemption (residence and remittance pages)

GOV.UK says that if your 2024 to 2025 return included the SA109 supplementary page, you're automatically exempt from MTD for the 2026 to 2027 tax year, and don't need to contact HMRC. You'll need to use MTD from 2027 to 2028 if your qualifying income is over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026.

If you didn't include the SA109 in 2024 to 2025 but reasonably expect to in your 2025 to 2026 or 2026 to 2027 return, GOV.UK says you can apply for an exemption. The reasons it lists for needing the SA109 include being:

  • non-resident in the UK in that tax year
  • UK resident and also tax resident in another country
  • eligible for overseas workday relief, or expecting split year treatment
  • eligible for the temporary repatriation facility or the foreign income and gains regime, and expecting to use it
  • a former remittance basis user in some situations (for example, expecting to remit more than £10 of nominated income or gains)
  • a non-UK resident foreign entertainer or sportsperson

GOV.UK also says not to apply if you don't have a good reason to think one of these will apply. Our MTD exemptions guide covers how applications work.

Source: GOV.UK, Find out if you can get an exemption from Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (updated 12 November 2026).

How records and quarterly updates work for foreign property

UK property businessForeign property business
What it coversAll your UK lettings, including your share of joint letsAll your overseas lettings, including your share of joint lets
Digital recordsNo need for separate records for each UK propertySeparate records for each foreign property (and for your share of each jointly let one)
Quarterly updatesOne per quarter for the whole UK businessOne per quarter for the whole foreign business
Income categories in the updateTotal rent; other income from property; lease premiums; reverse premiums and inducementsTotal rents and other receipts; lease premiums
Expense categories in the updateNine, from rent, rates and insurance to residential finance costs and travelOne: allowable property expenses
Simpler categorisationIf UK property turnover is under £90,000 (residential landlords still separate out some items)Allowed; GOV.UK says the £90,000 turnover threshold doesn't apply

Each update covers the tax year so far, and you still send one if nothing happened in the period. The deadlines (7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May) are the same for every business. So a landlord with UK and overseas rentals sends two updates each quarter, and a landlord who is also a sole trader sends three. For late updates, GOV.UK's penalty rules give one point per missed deadline however many businesses you have (see how MTD penalty points work).

For jointly let foreign property, HMRC's quarterly update direction lets you report income only in the quarterly updates and deal with expenses later, as for UK joint lets.

Sources: GOV.UK, Create digital records; GOV.UK, Send quarterly updates; HMRC, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: quarterly update direction.

What to check in software for foreign property

  • A separate foreign property business. It should send foreign property updates separately from your UK property business.
  • Records per property. GOV.UK requires separate digital records for each foreign property, then one combined update.
  • Joint lets abroad. Whether it records your share, and whether it lets you send income-only updates for joint lets.
  • Currency. Whether you can enter amounts in the local currency and how it converts them. GOV.UK's MTD guidance doesn't cover this.
  • The tax return. Whether it lets you submit the year-end return, including any claim for tax paid abroad, or whether you'd need a second product.
  • Your other income sources. UK property and self-employment, if you have them. See also free MTD software and the catches and bridging software for spreadsheets.

What GOV.UK doesn't say

  • How to convert foreign-currency amounts for quarterly updates: not covered in the MTD guidance pages.
  • Where foreign tax paid fits into MTD: the MTD pages don't mention it. The quarterly update categories for foreign property don't include a foreign tax line.
  • Whether a country breakdown is needed: the quarterly update direction lists only category totals for the foreign property business. Some software (for example Landlord Vision) asks you to set each property's country.
