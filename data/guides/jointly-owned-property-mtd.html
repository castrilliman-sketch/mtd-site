Short answer: under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax you only keep digital records of your share of jointly let property, your share counts towards the income threshold, and you can choose to leave expenses out of your quarterly updates for those properties, as long as you add them after the tax year ends.

Everything in the "what HMRC says" sections comes from GOV.UK pages we read on 19 November 2026, linked beside each point. Software details come from each provider's own website, read the same day. This page sets out the options; it doesn't say which to use.

What HMRC says about jointly let property

QuestionWhat GOV.UK says
Whose figures go in my records?You only need records for your share of income and expenses from jointly let properties.
Does it count towards the MTD threshold?Yes, your share of the property income counts towards your qualifying income.
Do I link records with the other owner?No, you do not need to link your digital records to the other landlord's.

How your share counts towards the threshold

HMRC's own example: you jointly own a property with your sibling that makes £50,000 of income, you each receive an equal share, and you have no self-employment income. Your qualifying income is £25,000. If you only learn your share after expenses have been deducted, HMRC says it will assess that figure for qualifying income.

Your share of jointly let property is added to any other self-employment and property income you have before the threshold is applied. Our Do I need MTD? page shows the £50,000, £30,000 and £20,000 start dates, and our guide to signing up from April 2027 covers the £30,000 group.

Sources: GOV.UK, Work out your qualifying income; GOV.UK, Find out if and when you need to use MTD for Income Tax.

The two ways to handle expenses in quarterly updates

Option 1: income and expensesOption 2: income only
In each quarterly updateYour share of rent and your share of expensesYour share of rent only
After the tax yearNothing extra for these propertiesReport the expenses by resending your fourth quarterly update before you submit your tax return, or adjust the category total in your software

Two details are easy to miss. First, this relaxation covers jointly let properties only: GOV.UK says properties you own entirely still need both income and expenses in your quarterly updates. Second, if you take option 2 you do not need to create digital records for those joint expenses every quarter, but you still need them before you submit your tax return.

GOV.UK also says you may need to resend your fourth quarterly update to record joint property expenses that weren't in earlier updates. Your fourth update covers 6 April to 5 April (or 1 April to 31 March on calendar periods) and is due on 7 May.

Sources: GOV.UK, Send quarterly updates; GOV.UK, Keep digital records.

Records: one UK property business

GOV.UK says that if you have one or more UK properties they are legally treated as one "UK property business", so you don't need separate digital records for each UK property. Your share of any jointly let properties forms part of it, and compatible software adds the records together into one quarterly update.

If you know only your net income (after expenses), GOV.UK says to ask for the full income figure before expenses, then create a record for that amount and a separate record for the expenses.

For less detailed categories, GOV.UK allows simpler categorisation if total UK property turnover is below £90,000, and says this also applies to a landlord who jointly lets a property. If turnover reaches £90,000 you need to categorise records in full before you can send your quarterly update.

Source: GOV.UK, Keep digital records for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

Penalties in 2026 to 2027

GOV.UK says there are no penalties for missing a quarterly update deadline for the 2026 to 2027 tax year. The updates still need to be sent, and penalties for other things, such as a late tax return, still apply.
